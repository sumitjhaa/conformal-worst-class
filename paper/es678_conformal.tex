\begin{abstract}
Conformal prediction returns label sets that contain the truth with probability $\ge 1-\alpha$ for any classifier, but the
guarantee averages over classes and over calibration draws. On MNIST, CIFAR-10 (two architectures), CIFAR-100 and ImageNet we
compare 13 methods under a leakage-free four-way split. Every method is marginally valid, yet the worst class is far below target:
\WorstINetNaiveT\% on ImageNet for the smallest-set score. We derive an exact law for the worst class under class-wise calibration;
it matches exchangeable re-splits to within \NsMaxErr\ points from 10 to 1{,}000 classes, flags a per-class shift in CIFAR-100's
official test set, and shows per-class guarantees need thousands of images per class at ImageNet scale. A high-probability correction shared across clusters of similar classes (Clustered-PAC) lifts
ImageNet's worst class to \WorstINetClusPAC\% with \SizeINetClusPAC\ labels per set, where the per-class version must return all 1{,}000.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and Motivation}
We take a \textbf{fixed, trained} image classifier $\hat p(y\mid x)$ over $K$ classes. Only the image is seen at prediction time. We
want a set $C(x)\subseteq\{1,\dots,K\}$ with $P(Y\in C(X))\ge1-\alpha$; one row is one image with its label, and the target is the set.
All valid methods meet this bar, so we ask: (i) how small are the sets; (ii) how far does \textbf{class-conditional} coverage
$P(Y\in C\mid Y=c)$ fall below $1-\alpha$, given that the marginal guarantee does not control it; (iii) can the worst class be
predicted, and repaired at what cost? We claim nothing for a single image, under shift, or about causes.

\section{Data and Preparation}
MNIST (LeNet), CIFAR-10 (small ResNet; and an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 as a second architecture), CIFAR-100 (small ResNet), all
from \texttt{torchvision}; ImageNet via published ResNet-152 softmax outputs on its 50k validation images
\citep{angelopoulos2021uncertainty}. Per seed the official training set is split into disjoint $D_\text{train}/D_\text{design}/D_\text{cal}$
(80/10/10\%) and the official test set is $D_\text{test}$, used once; ImageNet is re-split 20/40/40\% (about 20 calibration images per
class). An assertion checks disjointness. Normalisation, weights and class Gaussians are fit on $D_\text{train}$; every tuned quantity
uses $D_\text{design}$ only; augmentation is train-only. Classes are balanced. Shift sets: rotation/noise, CIFAR-10.1
\citep{recht2018cifar}, CIFAR-10-C \citep{hendrycks2019benchmarking}; unfamiliar inputs: FashionMNIST/KMNIST, CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10. SVHN was
unreachable and dropped. Seeds: \SeedsMNIST/\SeedsCTen/\SeedsCTenR/\SeedsCHun/\SeedsINet; test accuracy \AccMNIST, \AccCTen, \AccCTenR,
\AccCHun\ and \AccINet\%.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Baseline:} the top-1 label (no guarantee). \textbf{Split conformal:} for a score $s$, $k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$,
$\hat q=S_{(k)}$ ($\infty$ if $k>n$), $C(x)=\{y:s(x,y)\le\hat q\}$ \citep{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution}.
\textbf{Scores:} naive $1-\hat p$ (smallest sets, \citealp{sadinle2019least}), margin, APS \citep{romano2020classification}, RAPS
\citep{angelopoulos2021uncertainty}, SAPS \citep{huang2024saps}, a score learned for small sets \citep{stutz2022learning}, temperature
scaling \citep{guo2017calibration}, and our first proposal, $s+\gamma\tilde d_M$ with the Mahalanobis feature distance \citep{lee2018simple}.
\textbf{Grouping:} Mondrian (one $\hat q_c$ per class, \citealp{vovk2012conditional}); Clustered (one $\hat q$ per cluster of classes with
similar $D_\text{design}$ score quantiles, \citealp{ding2023class}); and their \textbf{PAC} versions, which use the smallest $k$ with
$P(\mathrm{Beta}(k,n_g{+}1{-}k)\ge1-\alpha)\ge1-\delta_g$, $\delta_g=1-(1-\delta)^{1/G}$, so that all $G$ groups are covered at once with
probability $\ge1-\delta$ ($\delta=0.1$). Clustered-PAC is our proposed repair. \textbf{Shift repairs:} ACI \citep{gibbs2021adaptive},
covariate-shift weighting \citep{tibshirani2019conformal} and label-shift weighting with BBSE weights
\citep{podkopaev2021distribution,lipton2018detecting}. \textbf{Tuning} ($T$, $\lambda$, $\gamma$, the learned score, clusters) uses
$D_\text{design}$ only, so every score is fixed before $D_\text{cal}$ and keeps the guarantee.

\section{Evaluation Design}
On $D_\text{test}$ for $\alpha\in\{0.01,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$, mean over seeds: coverage (validity); mean/90th-percentile size, singleton and
empty rates (efficiency); worst-class coverage, size-stratified violation and the worst of 10 feature clusters (conditional validity);
coverage by confidence decile, under label shift and covariate shift; set-size mix on unfamiliar inputs. Paired differences carry 95\%
hierarchical bootstrap intervals (seeds, then test images). Accuracy is context only.

\section{Graduate Depth Analysis}
\textbf{Derivation.} With exchangeable, a.s.\ distinct scores the test score's rank among $n{+}1$ is uniform, so
$P(Y\in C)=k/(n{+}1)\in[1-\alpha,1-\alpha+\tfrac1{n+1})$; given $D_\text{cal}$, coverage is $F(S_{(k)})\sim\mathrm{Beta}(k,n{+}1{-}k)$
\citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}. At $n=100$, \BelowHundred\% of 200 re-splits fall below $1-\alpha$.
\textbf{Proposition 1 (worst-class law).} Under class-wise calibration the calibration sets of different classes are disjoint, so per-class
coverages are independent $\mathrm{Beta}(k_c,n_c{+}1{-}k_c)$, observed on $m_c$ test images as Beta--binomials, and
$P(\min_c\widehat{\mathrm{cov}}_c\ge t)=\prod_cP(\widehat{\mathrm{cov}}_c\ge t)$. Predicted vs measured Mondrian worst class: CIFAR-10
\PredCTenMond/\MeasCTenMond, CIFAR-100 \PredCHunMond/\MeasCHunMond, ImageNet \PredINetMond/\MeasINetMond\%. On pooled re-splits
(exchangeable by construction) it is within \NsMaxErr\ points everywhere; CIFAR-100's official test set sits \MissPtsCHun\ points
below it (Fisher $p=\FisherCHun$ over seeds), a per-class train/test shift \citep{recht2018cifar} that the law detects. Requiring every class within 2 points w.p.\ 0.9 needs \NeedTen, \NeedHun\ and \NeedThou\ images per class
for $K=10,100,1000$. The same independence makes the PAC level $\delta_g$ exact (no union bound) and gives the feasibility limit
$n_g\ge\lceil\log\delta_g/\log(1-\alpha)\rceil$ (\PacMinINet\ on ImageNet).
\textbf{Marginal $\ne$ conditional, and label shift.} Coverage is $\sum_c\pi_c\mathrm{cov}_c$, so under a new class prior $\pi'$ it can fall
to $\min_c\mathrm{cov}_c$. Under strong Dirichlet label shift on CIFAR-10, Naive($T$)'s worst draw is \LsCTenNaiveMin\% while Mondrian-PAC's is
\LsCTenMondPACMin\%. Exact object-conditional coverage is impossible with informative sets \citep{vovk2012conditional,barber2021limits}.
\textbf{Failure audit.} On CIFAR-10.1 APS($T$) covers \TenCTenAPST\%; at CIFAR-10-C severity 5, \CcCTenAPSTSFive\%. ACI restores the most
severe segment of a drifting stream from \AciCTenFixCov\% to \AciCTenAciCov\% by growing sets (\AciCTenFixSize$\to$\AciCTenAciSize\ labels);
covariate-shift weighting lifts coverage from \WtCovStdCTen\% to \WtCovWCTen\%, but only by returning every label for \WtInfCTen\% of images, because shifted and clean images barely overlap. The distance score helps little because
the softmax already ranks the deciding (second) label better (AUROC \DdSoftCTen\ vs \DdDistCTen).

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
The worst-class law covers class-wise calibration under class-wise exchangeability; Clustered-PAC protects clusters, not individual classes,
and its clusters come from a small design split. ImageNet seeds re-split one model's outputs; shifts are synthetic or benchmark corruptions.
\textbf{Conclusion.} The score decides who is covered and the grouping decides which classes are protected. The worst class is predictable
in advance, needs thousands of images per class to guarantee individually, and is cheaply protected with high probability by sharing
corrected thresholds across similar classes. Report the worst class and the empty-set rate next to marginal coverage.
